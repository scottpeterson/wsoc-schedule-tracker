What happened

North Central (IL), ranked 13th, was scheduled to play at UChicago, ranked 21st, on Wednesday, September 30, 2026 at 4 PM Central on the grass field at Stagg Field. UChicago had announced on September 22 that the game would stream on the FloSports Channel through Prime Video and on FloCollege, its first nationally distributed broadcast and the first University Athletic Association game on Prime Video. Both teams were unbeaten at the time of that announcement. UChicago leads the series 9-0-1 and won 2-1 in Naperville in 2025.

It rained in Chicago through the day. UChicago published an article, time-stamped 11:01 AM Central, saying the North Central coaching staff had decided not to travel, that UChicago had prepared both the grass field and the artificial turf, and that the game would be recorded as a forfeit win for UChicago. At 12:42 PM, North Central posted that the game was postponed because of weather. At 1:37 PM, UChicago posted its article on X with a "GAME FORFEITED" graphic. North Central has not commented further. The NCAA's scoreboard lists the game as canceled.

What the NCAA rules say

Four NCAA documents cover a game that is not played. The playing rules and the statistics policies apply to every division. Each quote below links to the PDF it comes from.

Playing rules: a game that never starts is a no contest

The 2026 and 2027 NCAA Men's and Women's Soccer Rules Book (updated August 10, 2026) covers both outcomes in Rule 7.6, "No Contest and Forfeit":

7.6.1 When a scheduled game is terminated before it begins, it is recorded as "no contest." Neither team is credited with a win or loss, regardless of the reason why the game was terminated.

7.6.2 If a game is terminated by the officials during play for violations of Rule 5.6.5, the following procedures shall apply. If 70 minutes have not been completed, the statistics shall not count, and all cards are voided, except for violent behavior II, fighting and referee assault red cards, which are subject to the associated penalties in Rule 12. The final score of a game under these circumstances shall be recorded as 1-0 to the nonoffending team. If 70 minutes have been completed, and the nonoffending team is in the lead, the score of the game when terminated becomes the final score. If the offending team is leading or the game is tied, the final score shall be recorded as 1-0 to the nonoffending team. If 70 minutes have been completed, all statistics, including cards, shall count and the appropriate penalties shall be served.

Rule 5.6.5 lists the only in-game situations that produce a forfeit: a coach who prolongs a discussion with an official or refuses to leave the field, a team that refuses to return to the field within three minutes, or an ejected player who reenters as a substitute. The penalty reads: "The game is terminated and a forfeit is awarded to the opposing team. See Rule 7.6.2."

A team that does not show up falls under Rule 5.6.6, which tells the referee to:

5.6.6 Declare a "no contest" when a team without prior notification is not on the field and prepared to play within 15 minutes after the contracted starting time, unless evidence of extenuating circumstances can be provided.

Rule 12.9.5.1 adds: "Regular season games that are cancelled or result in a no contest do not count for serving a suspension."

Statistics policy: a school cannot declare a forfeit for a game that was not played

The NCAA Statistics Policies and Guidelines, the document sports information directors follow when they report records to the NCAA, has a "No Contest" Declaration on page 23:

There is no forfeit of a contest until all participating teams are present and the referee or other appropriate contest official has assumed jurisdiction in accordance with the applicable playing rules. When a team does not appear (e.g., due to weather conditions, accidents, breakdown of vehicles, illness or catastrophic circumstances, shortage of players), a forfeit is not recorded unless the rules of the sport provide that option. An institution shall not, for statistical purposes, declare a forfeit for nonfulfillment of a contest. Such instances shall be considered as "no contest." In circumstances involving institutions from the same conference, the league office has the option to declare a forfeit win and loss for conference-standings purposes only, but this does not change an institution's overall won-lost record. The "no contest" declaration came into effect at the start of the 1977-78 season.

The same document sets the recorded score of a soccer forfeit at 1-0 and the "reasonable point of conclusion" for statistics to count at 70 minutes (page 22). The 3-0 score that UChicago's page showed briefly on September 30 is not the NCAA forfeit score for soccer.

The NCAA Soccer Statisticians' Manual repeats the policy as Article 7 of its official scoring section, with one change in wording: "An institution shall not, for statistical purposes, declare a forfeit for nonfulfillment of a contract."

Championship manual: NPI counts only contests that are played

The 2025-26 NCAA Division III Soccer Pre-Championship Manual defines the NCAA Power Index (NPI) inputs in Appendix D. Winning percentage is "A team's number of wins divided by the number of that team's total contests," and strength of schedule is the average of the opponents' NPI. For women's soccer the weights are 20 percent winning percentage and 80 percent strength of schedule, with a quality-win bonus above an NPI of 54.00 and a minimum of 8 wins. The manual has no provision for forfeits or no contests in the regular season. The only forfeit it mentions is for exceeding the 24-player squad limit during the championship. A no contest therefore adds nothing to either team's NPI. The 2026-27 manual had not been posted as of October 1, 2026.

How the rules apply here

Under Rule 7.6.1 and the statistics policy, a game that ends before it begins is a no contest for NCAA records, whatever the reason, and the host cannot record a forfeit win. The conference-standings exception does not apply because North Central plays in the CCIW and UChicago plays in the UAA. This matches what the NCAA scoreboard shows (canceled, no box score) and what UChicago's own schedule page shows: its 7-1 record does not include a forfeit win. The rules do not stop a school from describing the game as a forfeit on its schedule page or in a press release, which is where the two schools still differ. Whether a team that declines to travel owes the host anything is a matter of the game contract between the schools, which neither school has published.
